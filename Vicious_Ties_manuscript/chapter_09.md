# Chapter 9: Blood and Reckoning

Inside the main warehouse office at Pier 14, Victor Moretti sat pinned against his mahogany desk, surrounded by Vance's armed tactical operators. 

Moretti was bleeding from a head wound, his custom Italian silk suit torn, his eyes wide with desperate, animal terror. The warehouse outside was silent, Moretti's syndicate guards completely disarmed and secured by Marcus Kane's strike team. Heavy rain began to patter against the corrugated tin roof above, creating a steady, metallic rhythm in the quiet harbor.

Killian walked into the office slowly, holding Nora tightly by the hand. He had wiped the blood from his knuckles, but his silver-blue eyes burned with an icy, absolute authority that dominated the room. He radiated the dark power of a man who owned the night and everything within it.

"Killian... please!" Moretti whimpered, holding up his bleeding hands. "It was business! Just business! I'll give you the European container ports! I'll pay fifty million dollars in unmarked bearer bonds! I'll sign over my shipping fleets in Marseilles!"

Killian didn't say a word. He walked over to the desk, standing over the rival boss like an executioner delivering an absolute sentence. His towering frame filled the room with cold, suffocating dread.

"You killed Arthur Mercer," Killian said softly, his voice colder than the ocean fog outside. "A good man who had a daughter who loved him."

Moretti looked at Nora, his eyes pleading for mercy. "Girl! Tell him! I'll pay you whatever you want! Millions! I'll set up a charitable foundation in your father's name! Just don't let him kill me! I have children!"

Nora stepped forward beside Killian. She looked down at the man who had ordered her father's death, feeling no fear—only a quiet, righteous closure. She squeezed Killian's hand, feeling the steady strength of the man who had protected her life.

"My father's memory isn't for sale, Moretti," Nora said clearly, her voice steady and resolute. "You took a gentle man's life because you were greedy and ruthless. You thought your power protected you from consequences, but your empire ends tonight. You will spend the rest of your days behind bars."

Killian looked down at Nora, his hand resting on his holstered weapon. "Do you want me to end it, Nora? Say the word, and he vanishes into the ocean."

Nora met Killian's silver eyes. She saw his willingness to burn the world for her, but she also saw the man she loved—a man who deserved to step out of the shadows.

"He faces justice, Killian," Nora said gently, placing her hand over his. "Federal prosecutors have the flash drive. Let the Feds take him to federal prison for the rest of his miserable life. We win by destroying his empire legally."

Killian stared at her for a long moment, amazed by her mercy, her intelligence, and her wisdom. He lowered his weapon.

"Marcus," Killian commanded smoothly. "Call the FBI Special Agent in Charge. Tell him Victor Moretti is gift-wrapped on Pier 14."

Moretti sagged against the desk, sobbing as Marcus cuffed his wrists and led him out into the fog.

Killian turned to Nora, taking both of her hands in his.

Out on the foggy pier, as police sirens wailed in the distance, Killian dropped down onto one knee before her on the wet asphalt.

Nora gasped, her hands flying to her mouth.

"Killian..."

"Six months ago, Nora," Killian said, his dark, dangerous voice shaking with raw vulnerability, "I was a dead man ruling a kingdom of shadows. I thought power was the only thing that could keep me safe. Then you walked into my life with a stolen decrypter, a loaded gun, and a heart full of fire... and you gave me a reason to live."

He pulled a velvet box from his coat pocket, opening it to reveal a stunning six-carat emerald-cut diamond ring flanked by black diamonds.

"I gave you the evidence to walk away," Killian murmured, looking up into her amber eyes. "You are completely free, Nora. You can leave this island, write your book, and live a quiet life. But I am begging you, Little Bird... stay with me. Rule my empire with me. Be my wife."

Nora's tears overflowed, spilling down her cheeks. She dropped to her knees in front of him on the wet pier, throwing her arms around his neck, kissing him with all the passion and devotion in her soul.

"Yes," Nora cried out over the sound of foghorns and sirens. "Yes, Killian! A thousand times yes!"
